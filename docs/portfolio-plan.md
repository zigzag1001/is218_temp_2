# Portfolio Plan

This is the evolving public-facing portfolio record. The student's full career comparison, personal eligibility assessment, and reflection belong in private Canvas; the working hypotheses below are not claims about the student's experience or preferences.

## Two directions to explore

| Direction | Work and people served | Possible portfolio evidence | Learning question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forward-deployed engineering | Work with customer stakeholders to turn an operational need into deployed software; see [Palantir](employers.md#1-forward-deployed-engineering-palantir). | A project story showing the user problem, contribution, decision, and checked outcome. | How can customer feedback become a testable requirement? |
| Applied AI software engineering | Build and evaluate AI-enabled software for users or internal teams; see [Anthropic and ICE](employers.md). | A real implementation and evaluation or a clearly labeled future experiment, never an invented AI feature. | How are model outputs checked and failures communicated? |

**Working direction:** emphasize customer-facing software delivery while leaving room to explore AI implementation. This is a provisional editorial recommendation for the student to accept or revise after private reflection. The tradeoff is a narrower initial story than an AI-specialist portfolio. The sources describe particular roles, not the student's qualifications.

## Reader and message

**Reader:** an early-career technical recruiter or engineering reviewer.

**Message:** show what was actually built, who it was for, the student's own contribution, and evidence of decisions and checks. The creative game is currently a design and research record, not a finished or AI-powered app. The group project's actual contributions and status still need to be identified.

## Two design directions

| Direction | Shape | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence-first case studies | Compact introduction followed by individual and group project stories with code, checks, and limits. | Makes contribution and proof easy to find. | Less visual storytelling on the first screen. |
| Visual project gallery | Prominent project imagery followed by expandable project details. | Makes creative work easier to browse quickly. | Reviewers may miss contribution or verification behind extra clicks. |

**Working choice:** evidence-first case studies for the technical reviewer; add imagery where it helps explain real work. A peer should try both lightweight layouts before this choice is treated as validated. Neither layout has been built or tested.

## Portfolio stories and planned checks

1. As a reviewer, I can identify the student's direction and find the individual project and group contribution from the first page. **Check:** ask a peer to locate each without coaching.
2. As a reviewer, I can open a project story and distinguish the student's contribution, decision, and current status. **Check:** ask the peer to explain what the student actually did.
3. As a reviewer, I can follow links to the relevant repository and any real tests, issues, or demonstrations, with unfinished work labeled. **Check:** open each link and compare claims against the linked evidence.
4. As a reviewer, I can find the student's real GitHub, LinkedIn, readable résumé, and professional contact route. **Check:** verify each destination and remove placeholders before publication.
5. As a reviewer, I can navigate the portfolio on a phone and with a keyboard. **Check:** inspect layout and complete navigation on both; not yet run.

The finished portfolio is a later stage, not a Plan requirement. Keep the same record as actual project evidence and feedback become available.
